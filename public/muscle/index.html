Part one · The hallway

Arun, age 16

Fifty kilos in his school shoes. Every morning, the same three guys waited by the lockers.

The shove never hurt as much as the laughing did, and nobody else in the hallway ever said anything.

Part two · The mirror

That night

He pulled off his shirt and looked properly for the first time: ribs, narrow shoulders, wrists like pencils.

He typed “how to get big fast” into his phone. The results were full of miracle powders, 30-day transformations and shouting men. None of it made sense to him.

Myth #1

“Some people are just born skinny. They can’t build muscle.”

The truth

Genetics affect how fast you build muscle and where it shows first. They don’t decide whether you can.

Most self-described “hardgainers” are really under-eaters. When someone eats enough, gets enough protein and adds a little more weight or a few more reps over time, the body responds. That’s true for people of every body type.

Arun didn’t know that yet.

Part three · The floor

Three

He got down on his bedroom floor and lowered himself.

One. Two. On the third his arms shook so badly he collapsed onto the floor.

Three push-ups. He lay on the floor and laughed, because it was the first number he could write down. Everything after this gets measured against it.

Part four · The decision

No more shortcuts

The next morning he woke at six. He skipped the powders and the 30-day promises and wrote one sentence in a notebook:

“Do a little more than yesterday. Eat like it matters. Sleep.”

He didn’t know it yet, but that sentence covers almost everything that matters. From here, you train with him.

Chapter 1 · The foundation

How muscle actually grows

Arun’s notebook sentence is the science. Nearly all muscle growth comes from three things, and everything else is detail on top of them.

01

Progressive overload

Muscle grows when you give it a reason to. That means doing a bit more over time: one more rep, slightly more weight, or a harder variation of the exercise.

Target: roughly 10–20 hard sets per muscle group each week, finishing each set with only 1–3 reps left in the tank.
02

Fuel

Your body can’t build tissue out of nothing. You need enough protein and a small calorie surplus. That doesn’t mean eating huge amounts.

Target: about 1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg of bodyweight each day, plus around 250–500 calories above maintenance.
03

Recovery

Training is the signal, and growth happens while you rest. If you skip sleep, you lose a lot of what the workout earned you.

Target: 7–9 hours of sleep and at least 48 hours before training the same muscle hard again.
Your first step

Week 1: Arun’s bedroom workout

You don’t need a gym, only some floor space and a chair. Train 3 days a week with a rest day between sessions, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday.

  • Use the easiest version of each exercise where you can do 8 clean reps.
  • Rest 60–90 seconds between sets.
  • Write down your reps every session. That notebook is your progress.
  • Once you hit the top of a rep range on every set, move to a harder variation.
